PVJets is a European broker but its operational network is global. For an SDR this matters for two reasons: (1) when a client asks for a route, you should know whether it's "home turf" or a niche route; (2) response-time expectations vary depending on the client's timezone.

## The operational model

PVJets does not own aircraft. What it owns is a **network of partner operators and handlers** spread across key markets. Every route is served by coordinating the most suitable aircraft at that moment — operator with availability, competitive cost, adequate safety certifications.

In concrete terms:

- More than **3,600 aircraft** accessible in the global network (number cited for On-Demand Charter)
- Partnerships with operators certified IS-BAO / Argus / Wyvern (see module 2)
- **Empty Legs** distributed across the entire network — already-planned repositioning flights PVJets fills at a discount

The competitive advantage is the combination **dense network + advisor who knows it**. Just having access to 3,600 aircraft isn't enough — you need to know which, when, and why.

This is the map you'll see most often in chat. Almost every incoming request has a European city on at least one side (origin or destination).

## How this applies to your work

When a request arrives, before passing it to the broker, ask yourself three quick questions:

1. **Are origin and destination in the PVJets network?** Most of the time, yes. If it's a very unusual destination (e.g. a secondary airport in a country not on the list), flag it as "verify operational access".
2. **Which macro-area?** Internal Europe, transatlantic, intercontinental. The likely aircraft type — and therefore price — change.
3. **If it's a seasonal destination, are we in peak?** If yes, cite in the handoff "peak season — verify slot" (Ibiza August, Innsbruck Christmas, Mykonos July, etc.).
