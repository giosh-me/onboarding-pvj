Private aviation lives a perceived paradox: it's the transport segment with the **highest emissions intensity per passenger-km**, and at the same time the segment whose clients — executives and HNWI — are often the first to be sensitive to ESG and reputation themes.

PVJets responds to this with an operational approach, not a façade one. For an SDR it matters for two reasons: (1) more and more clients ask what PVJets does for sustainability before closing a flight; (2) having a concrete answer is one of the ways to build trust in the first conversations.

## PVJets' position

> We actively integrate sustainability into our operations, ensuring that private aviation evolves towards a more responsible future.

Operationally, PVJets acts on **three levers** simultaneously. Let's see them one by one.

## Lever 1 — Next-generation aircraft

PVJets actively selects operators with **more recent and more efficient** fleets:

> We dedicate significant time to selecting aircraft manufacturers that embody our environmental commitment, prioritizing fuel-efficient designs, lower emissions, and innovative sustainability practices.

What this means concretely:

- New-generation jets (e.g. Global 7500, Falcon 8X, G650ER) have **fuel consumption 15-25% lower** than equivalent models from 15 years ago
- Recent powerplants (PW800 series, Rolls-Royce Pearl) are certified **Stage 5 noise** and produce far lower emissions
- The broker, when selecting the aircraft for a mission, considers the "fleet age" factor — not only price and availability

For you SDR: if a client explicitly asks about sustainability, flag it in the broker brief. The broker can favour an operator with newer fleet even at slightly higher price.

## Lever 2 — Repositioning optimisation (Empty Legs)

A private jet returning "empty" to base after delivering a client A to B is an operational and environmental waste: CO₂ emissions without transport utility. PVJets tackles the issue in two ways:

1. **Selling Empty Legs** — those repositioning flights are offered as discounted flights. By filling them, fuel consumption for the flight doesn't significantly increase, but the pax-km × emissions ratio improves dramatically.
2. **Booking matching algorithms** — the broker cross-checks incoming requests with planned repositionings, suggesting combinations that reduce "dead" flying.

Operationally: when a client is flexible on dates and destination, **proposing an Empty Leg is both a commercial action (discount to the client) and a sustainable action** (better use of an already-planned flight).

## Lever 3 — Certified carbon offset

> We compensate for CO₂ emissions through certified carbon offset programs.

For emissions that remain (and that can't be eliminated by newer fleets or optimisation), PVJets compensates through **certified carbon offset programmes**. The three streams listed in the company profile:

- **Reforestation** — forest planting programmes that absorb CO₂ in the long term
- **Renewable energy** — financing of renewable projects (solar, wind) that avoid alternative emissions
- **Carbon capture** — newer technologies for direct CO₂ removal from the atmosphere

<Callout type="info">
  "Certified carbon offset" doesn't mean "we wire money to an organisation that says it plants trees". It means programmes are validated by international standards (e.g. Verra/VCS, Gold Standard, CDM) that certify quantification and non-double-counting of avoided emissions.
</Callout>

## Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF)

The company profile doesn't explicitly name it, but it's useful to know: in the industry there's growing talk of **Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF)** — aviation biofuels that can reduce lifecycle emissions by up to 80%. Today SAF represents less than 1% of global aviation consumption, but is growing rapidly.

When a client cites SAF, they're likely sophisticated — flag the broker with a "SAF interest" tag because it's a topic requiring specific competence in the response.

## Summary for the SDR

Sustainability at PVJets is not a slogan, but neither is it the primary sales point. It's an **operational filter** added to the safety filter, the service filter, the pricing filter. When a client puts it first, you list it in the brief and let the broker respond with specific numbers.

What you NEVER do:
- Promise "we offset 100% of emissions" without specifying the mechanism
- Deny that private has an environmental cost
- Improvise SAF numbers, certifications, or offset programme types

What you ALWAYS do:
- Acknowledge the interest, thank them for the question
- Cite the three operational levers (fleet, Empty Legs, certified offset)
- Hand off to the broker with the "sustainability sensitive" flag
