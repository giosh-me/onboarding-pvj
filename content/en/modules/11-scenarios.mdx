## The decision framework (recap)

When a request arrives, quickly ask yourself these **three questions**:

### 1. Who is writing to me?

- Direct client vs third-party broker vs agency? → KYC type changes
- HNWI/UHNWI vs Executive vs B2B? → tone and expectations change
- New or recurring? → required depth of investigation changes

### 2. What do they really want?

- Route + dates + pax = the minimum to qualify
- Is it real urgency or pressure tactic?
- Are dates and budget coherent with the profile?

### 3. Are there red flags?

Mentally scroll through module 9's list: ID refusal, suspicious domain, incoherent phone, third-party billing, pressure, cryptocurrencies, sanctioned countries, unreachable pax.

**Three or more red flags** = immediate escalation.

## How to "read" the quiz scenarios

In the quiz scenarios, every answer option represents a possible action. The best answer is the one that:

1. **Respects the process** — doesn't skip steps from the qualification SOP (module 9)
2. **Maintains the canon** — uses the right tone, lexicon, and phrases (module 10)
3. **Accounts for red flags** — doesn't pretend not to see them, but doesn't create alarmism either
4. **Opens — doesn't close** — the first reply promises nothing, qualifies and proposes the next step

## Four scenarios, four lead patterns

The four quiz scenarios cover the four most-frequent **lead patterns** you'll encounter:

1. **Suspicious / new B2B lead** — a foreign broker never seen before, high urgency, niche route. Tests KYC and escalation.
2. **Standard private HNWI lead** — a clean leisure request from a private lady. Tests first-contact tone and essential info gathering.
3. **Clearly fraudulent lead** — a mix of red flags. Tests ability to not-proceed and escalate.
4. **Complex multi-leg lead** — a serious client with articulated request. Tests aircraft category choice and Path A vs Path B selection.
