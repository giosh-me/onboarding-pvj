# Aircraft types in detail

In module 5 you saw the **six official categories** of the PVJets company profile. This module expands that picture in two directions:

1. **Adds the VLJ** (Very Light Jet), a category not in the company profile but present in the market and that you may encounter in client conversation
2. **Turns the encyclopedia into decisions**: how, faced with a client request, to map the likely category in 30 seconds

Goal: when the broker opens your handoff, they should find a category proposal already framed, not a list of pax and km to decode.

## The seven categories (including VLJ)

### 0. VLJ — Very Light Jet (entry level)

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Pax | 4-6 |
| Range | ~2,000-2,500 km |
| Typical flight | 1.5-2.5 hours |
| Examples | Phenom 100, Citation Mustang, HondaJet Elite, Eclipse 550 |

VLJ is the **entry-level category** of jet private aviation. Lower hourly costs, narrower cabins, no own lavatory (or very small). PVJets does not promote them directly because the cut is "premium/luxury" and typical clients want at least Super Light. **However**, a new client asking "what's the cheapest for a short route?" might receive a VLJ option — the broker decides.

<Aside>
  You'll see this rarely, but if a client mentions Phenom 100 or HondaJet, know they're talking about VLJ. The conversation typically ends with the broker proposing a Super Light (Citation XLS, Phenom 300) for about 30-40% more hourly cost but with cabin, lavatory, and superior range.
</Aside>

### 6. VIP Airliner — the luxury group charter

For 18+ pax group charter or for those who explicitly want airliner-format cabin. Examples: Boeing BBJ (737-based), Airbus ACJ319/320 (A319/A320-based), Lineage 1000 (modified Embraer 190).

### 7. Helicopters

PVJets operates helicopter transfers for:
- **Short urban transfers** (Nice-Monaco, Milan-Lugano)
- **Access to yachts / villas with heliports**
- **Event flight peaks** (motorsport, regatta, gala)

Typical partner-operator models: AgustaWestland AW139, Airbus H145, Bell 429.

## Decision tree: how to map mission → category

When a request arrives, in 30 seconds answer these three questions in order:

### Question 1 — Distance/flight duration

| Estimated duration | Likely categories |
|---|---|
| Under 1.5h | Heli (urban) or VLJ |
| 1.5-3h | VLJ - Super Light |
| 3-4h | Midsize |
| 4-5h | Super Midsize |
| 5-7h | Heavy Jet |
| 7-9h | Heavy Jet or ULR (with technical stop) |
| 9h+ | Ultra Long Range non-stop |

### Question 2 — Pax (and bags)

| Pax | Categories |
|---|---|
| 1-4 | Any category, but cost/pax jumps on high categories |
| 5-8 | Super Light, Midsize, Super-mid |
| 9-12 | Super-mid, Heavy |
| 13-16 | ULR, Heavy with extended range |
| 17+ | VIP Airliner |

### Question 3 — Special constraints

- **Secondary airport or short runway?** → Pilatus PC-24, possibly VLJ
- **Seasonal peak (Innsbruck Christmas, Mykonos July)?** → flag broker for slot
- **UHNWI client with known preference?** → cross-check CRM if there's an indication
- **Route with technical stops (e.g. Milan-Buenos Aires)?** → ULR for non-stop, or Heavy with one technical fuel stop

## Operational summary for the SDR

When you hand a brief to the broker, the "aircraft indication" line must be **a category proposal + a disclaimer**:

> "Expecting Super Midsize (Challenger 300/350 or Praetor 600) — verify availability + client preference on stop vs non-stop"

Not:
- "Super Midsize" (too dry, doesn't help the broker)
- "Challenger 350" (you can't promise a model)
- "Let's see what the operator says" (you didn't do triage)

That mid-level synthesis is the **value-add** output of the SDR. The broker saves time, the client gets the quote sooner.
