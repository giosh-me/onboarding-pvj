# Our fleet

PVJets does not own a fleet itself, but offers aircraft from a network of **3,600+ aircraft** worldwide organised in **six categories**. Each category has different range, pax capacity, comfort level and hourly cost. Recognising at a glance which category fits a request is the **first operational skill** that distinguishes a sharp SDR from a generic one.

You don't need to become an aeronautical engineer. You need to recognise quickly: this route + this number of pax + these bags = most likely category X.

## The six categories

**When to use it**: intra-Europe trips up to 3 hours (e.g. Milan-Mykonos, Rome-Marrakech, Paris-Madrid). It's the most-requested category in the European market by volume: small groups, contained hourly cost, excellent agility on regional airports.

It's used for **group charter** (sports delegations, music groups, large corporate teams, diplomatic processions) and for those who really want airliner-format cabin with luxury fittings.

## Helicopter Transfer

PVJets also serves **helicopter transfers** — typically short city-to-city links or access to destinations with private heliports (yacht in open sea, private villas, resorts with heliport). Specifics on helicopter models are not in the company profile and vary by route — handled case-by-case with the broker.

Typical heli request examples:
- Nice ↔ Monaco (5 minutes vs an hour of traffic)
- Milan-Linate ↔ Lugano
- Lake Como heliport ↔ Linate airport
- Transfer to Cuore (yacht/villa) from airport

## How to read a request as an SDR

When a message arrives like *"I'd need a flight Rome → New York for 8 people with bags on March 18"*, mentally do this map:

1. **Distance/duration** → Rome-NY ~8 hours → we're in **Heavy Jet** (5-7h tight limit, 8h is borderline) or more likely **Ultra Long Range** non-stop without technical stop
2. **Pax** → 8 people → cabins OK for both categories (Heavy 10-12, ULR 14-16)
3. **Bags** → suitcases OK on both
4. **Likely category** → Heavy Jet with possible technical stop, or Ultra Long Range non-stop

The output you pass to the broker is not "category X". It's: "Rome-NY, 8 pax, March 18, expecting Heavy or ULR — verify availability + client preference on stop vs non-stop". That mid-level synthesis saves the broker time.

<Callout type="warning">
  Never promise a specific category to the client. The final choice depends on operator availability, slot, day's costs. You signal a "likely indication" to the broker — the broker confirms to the client.
</Callout>
