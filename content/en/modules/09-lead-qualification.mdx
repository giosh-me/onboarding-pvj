From here we enter the heart of the SDR craft. Module 9 gives you the **structured PVJets process** to qualify a lead — from the first information to gather to the broker handoff. It's not a "bureaucratic" procedure: it's the filter that protects you, the broker, and the company from weak leads and frauds.

The principles that run through everything:

1. **Never sacrifice due diligence for response speed**
2. **Every lead is a potential client but also a potential risk**
3. **If something seems too good to be true, it probably is**
4. **Document EVERYTHING in Salesforce**: every call, email, WhatsApp
5. **When in doubt, always escalate** to Team Lead or senior broker

## The 5 Salesforce flow phases

Every PVJets lead goes through five phases in order. Each has precise objectives and exit criteria:

| # | Phase | Objective | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Assigned** | Take ownership + initial anti-scam screening | Lead assigned, screening passed |
| 2 | **1st Contact** | Establish first contact, gather basic info | Reply received with minimum info |
| 3 | **2nd Contact** | Deepen the request + KYC Light | Identity verified, request confirmed |
| 4 | **Price Range Sent** | Send price range, evaluate reaction | Lead accepts range, wants to proceed |
| 5 | **Assign to Broker** | Structured broker handoff | Broker takes over |

**Cycle timing**: 48-72 hours for hot inbound leads. For outbound (cold prospecting) the cycle can extend to 7-10 days.

## Mandatory info to gather

Before you can pass a lead to the broker, you must have **all** these data registered in Salesforce:

### Contact
- Full first and last name (as on ID document)
- Email (no temporary emails like mailinator)
- Phone with country prefix (verified active)
- Company / Role (if business — cross-check on LinkedIn)

### Flight
- Departure airport / city (ICAO or city name)
- Arrival airport / city
- Requested dates (even just a range)
- Number of passengers (adults + children separately)
- Trip type (one-way / round-trip / multi-leg)

### Budget
- Budget indication or price expectations (even just a range)
- Previous experience with private jets (first-timer vs repeater) — recommended

### Identity
- ID document (passport / national ID) — **mandatory before broker handoff**
- Coherence verification of name/company/online profile (LinkedIn, company site)

<Callout type="warning">
  Without this information, you do NOT pass the lead to the broker. Period. You keep them at 1st Contact or 2nd Contact until you have it. A broker receiving an incomplete lead wastes time and rightly complains.
</Callout>

## Lead Scoring — the five criteria

To decide **handling priority**, every lead is scored on five axes (max 3 points, min 1 point, total 5-15):

| Criterion | ⭐ High (3 pt) | ⚠️ Medium (2 pt) | ❌ Low (1 pt) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Urgency** | Flight within 7 days | Flight within 30 days | Beyond 30 days / undefined date |
| **Info completeness** | All fields filled | Partial but essential fields | Only name + generic request |
| **Requester profile** | C-level, HNWI, known company | Verifiable professional | Profile unverifiable |
| **Experience** | Has flown private before | Knows the industry | Absolute first time |
| **Budget** | Aligned/above market | Slightly below market | Unrealistic or undeclared |

**Score interpretation**:
- **13-15 → HOT LEAD**: handle within **1 hour**
- **9-12 → WARM**: within **4 hours**
- **5-8 → COLD**: within **24 hours**
- **Under 5 → consider proceeding or archiving** with documented motivation

## Mandatory contact timings

| Channel | First contact | Follow-up | Max attempts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email | Within 30 min (business hours) | Every 24h | 3 in 7 days |
| Phone | Within 15 min | Every 24h, different time | 4 in 5 days |
| WhatsApp | Within 15 min | Every 12-24h | 3 in 5 days |

**Recommended follow-up cadence** if the lead doesn't respond:
- **D0**: first contact on preferred channel (default: phone + email)
- **D1**: second attempt on different channel
- **D2**: third attempt with value-added message (empty leg, route info)
- **D4**: fourth and last polite attempt
- **D7**: closing email — archive lead

## Anti-scam: the four fraud types

The industry is a frequent target for scams. Know the four categories:

### A. Credit card / payment fraud
Stolen or cloned cards. The lead asks to pay for third parties or refunds via different methods. May provide multiple cards in succession if the first is rejected.

### B. Identity theft / Impersonation
The fraudster impersonates a CEO, celebrity, executive. Emails similar but not equal to the corporate domain (e.g. `ceo@pvjets-corp.com` instead of `@pvjets.com`). Credible-looking social profiles but recently created.

### C. Money Laundering
Payments disproportionate to the route. Billing to entities other than the passenger. Payments from high-risk jurisdictions. Cancellations with refund to a different account from the original.

### D. Advance Fee / Social Engineering
Lead "interested" in a high-value flight asking YOU to advance costs. Proposes partnerships or commissions. Creates artificial urgency to bypass controls.

## Red Flags — the critical signals

<Callout type="warning">
  Three or more simultaneous red flags = **immediate escalation** to Team Lead.
</Callout>

### CRITICAL red flags — escalate immediately

- The lead **refuses** to provide ID document
- Email from **suspicious domain** (free for high-value business requests)
- Phone number **inconsistent** with declared country or unreachable
- Request for **billing to entity other than** the passenger without justification
- **Extreme pressure** to skip controls ("I have no time for these formalities")
- Payment proposal in **cryptocurrencies, vouchers, untraceable methods**
- Destination toward **sanctioned countries** (verify OFAC, EU, UN)
- The lead communicates on behalf of a passenger who is **never directly reachable**

### Yellow flags — investigate

- Email with free domain (Gmail, Yahoo) for high-value business requests
- LinkedIn profile absent, recent, or with few connections
- Inconsistent information between communications
- Refusal of voice/video calls (text only)
- Unrealistic budget (too high or too low)
- Asks lots of operational info without proceeding

## KYC Light — the identity verification procedure

For every lead that passes 1st Contact, before Price Range Sent you must:

1. **Document**: copy of passport/ID. Name matches, not expired, photo coherent.
2. **Digital**: search on LinkedIn, Google, company site. Profile must exist and be coherent.
3. **Phone**: at least one voice or video call to confirm identity.
4. **Business (B2B)**: verify company existence via business register; verify lead's role.
5. **Sanctions**: lead's name and company must not appear on OFAC SDN, EU Consolidated Sanctions List, UN Security Council, FATF High-Risk Jurisdictions.

Useful free tools: **OpenSanctions**, **Sanctions Explorer**.

## Request-profile coherence

Four incoherence signals requiring investigation:

| Signal | Possible explanation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Declared budget much > requested route | Laundering or testing reaction | Investigate; escalate |
| Route to conflict zones / sanctioned countries | Sanctions evasion | Consult lists; escalate immediately |
| Request on behalf of third parties without identifying end passenger | Fraud/laundering | Request actual ID; do not proceed |
| Frequent change of dates, destinations, pax | Indecision or testing | Document each change; verify coherence |

## Broker handoff

A lead can be passed to the broker ONLY if:

- All mandatory info is in Salesforce
- KYC Light completed with positive outcome
- Price Range sent and accepted
- No open red flags
- Adequate responsiveness (at least "Responsive")

**Handoff sheet** to compile in Salesforce:

- Request summary (route, dates, pax, type, special needs)
- Client profile (name, company, role, experience)
- Budget and expectations + reaction to price range
- Identity verification status (KYC Light outcome)
- Communication notes (preferred channel, hours, tone)
- Red/yellow flags detected and how handled
- Interaction history
- Recommendations to broker

The broker must have **all the context** to continue the conversation without the client having to repeat information.

## When to escalate

Always, if at least one of these is true:

- Three or more simultaneous red flags
- Match on sanctions list
- Suspected payment fraud
- Route to high-risk / sanctioned countries
- Lead aggressive or threatening when you ask for verification
- **Any situation where you don't feel safe proceeding** (this is explicit in the SOP — personal + procedural safety is priority)

**How to escalate**:
1. **Document** in Salesforce every red flag with date and details
2. **Communicate** to Team Lead via internal channel with a summary
3. **Suspend** the conversation until directed
4. **Don't alarm the lead**: use neutral phrasing like "we are finalising internal checks"

## Daily summary

Three things to remember every day:

1. **The 5 phases are linear**: don't skip a step to accelerate
2. **Red flags are cumulative**: 1-2 = investigate; 3+ = immediate escalation
3. **When in doubt, escalate**: the Team Lead exists for that, you're not alone
