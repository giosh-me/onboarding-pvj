## The four market actors

When a private flight reaches a client, behind it there are **four figures** that must be kept distinct. Confusion between these is one of the most frequent issues for new SDRs.

### Owner

The aircraft **owner**. Can be an individual, a company, a fund. Buys the jet, pays hangaring, maintenance, crew. They are the asset's "master".

### Operator

The one who **operationally manages** the jet — crew, maintenance, certifications, slots, navigation. The operator holds an **AOC** (Air Operator Certificate) authorising commercial aircraft operation. Owner and operator often coincide, but just as often an owner entrusts their jet to an external operator (the "managed aircraft" model).

### Broker

The one who **intermediates** between client and operator — owns no aircraft, has no AOC, but has **contact network, inventory access, and contractual capability** to organise flights. **PVJets is a broker.**

### Charterer / Client

The one who **rents** the flight for a single trip. The end client.

<Aside>
  Typical confusion: a new client says "I want to book with you the company". No. You're a broker, not an operator. The flight will be operated by a partner operator. It seems a wording issue but it matters for how you write the quote (and what you can/cannot promise on operations).
</Aside>

## Customer segments

In module 1 you saw the macro split **Executives vs HNWI**. Here we go one level deeper.

### Executives

Managers, directors, decision-makers using private flight as a **work tool**. Characteristics:

- **Tight calendar**: mid-week flights, often multi-leg in one day
- **Priority: time** — they want to land as close as possible to the actual destination
- **Privacy work**: they use the flight for meetings, laptop work, phone calls
- **Spend decided by company**: booking often comes via an assistant
- **High loyalty** if service is impeccable (switching provider is a cognitive cost)

### HNWI / UHNWI

Private clients with high spending power. The difference between HNWI and UHNWI is a wealth threshold:

- **HNWI** (High Net Worth Individual): investable wealth above ~$1M
- **UHNWI** (Ultra High Net Worth Individual): above ~$30M

Operational characteristics:

- **Leisure flights**: holidays, events, weekends, occasions
- **Priority: experience** — comfort, personalisation, exclusivity
- **Personal decision**: the client books in person or via personal assistant
- **Low price sensitivity in UHNWI, present in HNWI**
- **Frequent need for ancillary services**: concierge, luxury transfer, specific catering

### Family Office

For UHNWIs, often you don't talk to the client directly but to their **family office** — an asset/lifestyle management structure that handles all the family's wealth. The family office is itself a sophisticated client that knows the market. They handle everything professionally, ask for rigorous contracts, want full transparency.

### Charter operators for third parties

Sometimes the end clients are other **luxury travel agencies**, **hotel concierges**, **PR/event agencies**. They buy on behalf of the real client. For you SDR they are **B2B** clients: they want speed, competitive price, reliability.

## Commercial models in the market

Beyond the two PVJets models (On-Demand + Club), the global market offers other models worth knowing — because clients may cite them.

### Dry lease

The client rents the **aircraft "dry"**, that is without crew. They must provide their own pilots. Typical model for those with contracted pilots but who don't want to buy the jet. Rare in the luxury charter market.

### Wet lease

The **aircraft with crew**. That's what PVJets offers via On-Demand: you rent the jet with the operator's pilots. The standard charter model.

### Fractional ownership

The client buys a **fraction** of a jet (typically 1/16, 1/8, 1/4) and gets a number of flight hours per year. Dominant operators: NetJets, VistaJet (Program), Flexjet. Expensive model but with standardised service — direct competitor of PVJets Club for recurring UHNWIs.

### Jet card

The client buys **prepaid hours** in a programme at fixed rates. Typically 25-100 hours. Avoids the complexity of fractional. Operators: Sentient Jet, Wheels Up. It's primarily an American model.

### On-Demand Charter (PVJets)

What you sell. Pay-per-flight, real-time rate, no commitment. Lowest barrier-to-entry model.

### Membership (PVJets Club)

PVJets' prepaid model, alternative to fractional/jet card. You saw the detail in module 4.

## Operational vocabulary

Terms you'll find in chat, in broker handoffs, in industry conversations:

- **Positioning flight** — "repositioning" flight to bring the aircraft to the client's requested departure point. The client doesn't pay for it (the broker does).
- **Ferry flight** — synonym of positioning flight. Same thing, two names.
- **Empty Leg** — the positioning/ferry **sold** to third parties at a discount, because the flight will happen anyway.
- **FBO** (Fixed-Base Operator) — the **handler** that manages the aircraft on the ground (refuelling, hangaring, pax reception, customs). Examples: Signature, Universal Aviation, ExecuJet.
- **Slot** — takeoff/landing authorisation in a specific time window. At congested airports (Linate, Heathrow, Innsbruck in winter) slots are scarce and priced.
- **Handling** — ground services provided by the FBO.
- **AOC** (Air Operator Certificate) — the certification allowing an operator to operate commercially.
- **Pax** (Passengers) — the standard abbreviation you'll find everywhere.
- **N-reg / EU-reg** — aircraft registration (USA = N..., Europe = various prefixes). Has operational implications on slots and regulations.
- **VFR / IFR** — Visual / Instrument Flight Rules. Types of flight rules. For you: operational terminology you'll hear but won't use.
- **Catering** — onboard meal service. Must be specified per flight: standard, premium, kosher, vegan, etc.
- **De-icing** — aircraft de-icing before winter takeoff. Significant extra cost at Innsbruck/Salzburg/CDG-Le Bourget in January.

## For your SDR work

Three operational takeaways from this module:

1. **You know who you are**: PVJets = broker. Not operator. You state it in messages when relevant (e.g. "as a broker, we work with the network of top-tier operators...").
2. **You know who the client is**: from the first message, identify if they're executive, HNWI/UHNWI, family office, or agency. Tone changes, pricing changes, handoff priority changes.
3. **You know the vocabulary**: when the broker or a sophisticated client uses terms like "positioning", "FBO", "AOC", "fractional", you don't lose the thread.
